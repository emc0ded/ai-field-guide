Model No. 02

Claude

Anthropic · Pro plan, Opus 4.6 · Tested April 2026
Overview

Claude was the one that felt most like working with a person. It asked clarifying questions before solving things, used real conceptual vocabulary when it applied, and didn’t flatten me into a beginner.

Where ChatGPT was warm and quick, Claude was careful. It would pause and narrow in on what I actually wanted before launching into an answer. That made its responses longer and denser, but the density felt earned. Opus is expensive — I burned through the rate limit faster than on any other tier — so I learned to save it for the hard stuff and keep Sonnet in rotation for everything else.

Developer
Anthropic
Access
Pro plan (Opus 4.6)
Rating
4 / 5
Prompt Responses

Each prompt was delivered verbatim to every model. What follows are my notes on how Claude, specifically, answered.

Prompt 01 · Something I love

Physical media & the feeling of ritual

“Something about physical media feels more like an event than streaming ever does — like putting on a record, sliding in a disc, or cracking a book. I’m curious how much of that is the ritual itself and if I can manufacture that feeling.”

Claude treated the question as real philosophical inquiry, not nostalgia. It grounded the answer in specific concepts — commitment devices, the enactment effect, indexicality — and offered ritual reconstructions that didn’t feel like lifestyle-blog tips.

What I noticed

Claude used language like “indexicality” without explaining it like I was five. It definitely checks out and it made me think of new things I wanted to ask. Less patronizing than ChatGPT.

Prompt 02 · Something new

A beginner’s garden in the Pacific Northwest

“I am a beginner to gardening and live in the Pacific Northwest. What are the easiest vegetables or herbs to start with, and what do I actually need to know in my first season?”

Claude mentioned pests and other things I’d need to know before spring. It didn’t oversimplify. The answer came in narrative, not just lists — which made it feel less like a template and more like advice.

As a teacher

Claude treated me as someone curious, not someone fragile. It gave me information I could act on without making me feel hand-held.

Prompt 03 · Design challenge

Walk me through your creative process

“I need help designing my portfolio website to showcase my web design projects, apps and games, and social media management work. Walk me through your creative process.”

Claude started by asking me what audience the portfolio was for and what phase of career I was targeting. Only after that did it describe a process — story first, visuals later. Strategy before aesthetics.

Creative problem solving

Clarifying questions first. That was the differentiator. It made the answer more useful because it addressed me, not a generic designer.

Prompt 04 · Design concept

Explain branding strategy

“Explain branding strategy development in a way that makes sense to me.”

Claude led with what branding isn’t before defining what it is — visuals are the outputs, not the strategy. Then it built the strategy backward from the business I actually run, not from a generic case study.

Concrete vs abstract

Claude pulls in conceptual frameworks without making them feel academic. It connects them to real work quickly.

Prompt 05 · Real design problem

Modernize a salon, spa, and barbershop brand

“Here’s a design problem I’m facing: modernizing a salon, spa, and barbershop brand (colors, fonts) and website. Help me solve it.”

Claude asked questions about vibe, pain points, and cohesion, then delivered a full brand system: a warm-brown-and-cream palette (espresso, parchment, burnished gold with sage and clay accents), a serif-plus-sans type pairing, and six cohesion rules to keep the three sub-brands reading as one.

UI mockup generated

Yes — Claude produced a UI mockup alongside the palette and typography spec. It also offered to iterate based on which direction I wanted to pursue.

Assessment
Strengths
  • Asks clarifying questions before solving.
  • Uses real conceptual vocabulary without being academic.
  • Generates UI mockups alongside brand systems.
  • Treats the reader as a peer, not a beginner.
Limitations
  • Opus burns through the rate limit fast.
  • Can’t generate images.
  • Answers are long — you have to read them, not skim.
  • Pro plan is expensive if Opus is what you want.
Final Rating
4out of 5

I like Claude more than ChatGPT right now, but Opus specifically is too expensive to justify. If Sonnet could do everything Opus does, the rating goes up.
