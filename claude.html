Model No. 02

Claude

Anthropic · Pro plan, Opus 4.6 · Tested April 2026
Overview

Claude was the one that felt most like working with an actual person. It asked me things before solving them, used real conceptual vocabulary when it fit, and didn’t flatten me into a beginner.

Where ChatGPT was warm and quick, Claude was careful. It would pause and zero in on what I actually wanted before diving in. That made the responses longer and denser, but the density felt earned. Opus is expensive, I burned through the rate limit faster than on any other tier, so I learned to save it for the hard stuff and keep Sonnet in rotation for everything else.

Developer
Anthropic
Access
Pro plan (Opus 4.6)
Rating
4 / 5
Prompt Responses

Each prompt was delivered verbatim to every model. What follows are my notes on how Claude, specifically, answered.

Prompt 01 · Something I love

Physical media & the feeling of ritual

“Something about physical media feels more like an event than streaming ever does, like putting on a record, sliding in a disc, or cracking a book. I’m curious how much of that is the ritual itself and if I can manufacture that feeling.”

Claude treated the question as a real philosophical question, not nostalgia. It grounded the answer in specific concepts, commitment devices, the enactment effect, indexicality, and offered ritual setups that didn’t read like lifestyle-blog tips.

What I noticed

Claude used words like “indexicality” without explaining them like I was five. It checks out, and it made me think of new things I wanted to ask. Less patronizing than ChatGPT.

Prompt 02 · Something new

A beginner’s garden in the Pacific Northwest

“I am a beginner to gardening and live in the Pacific Northwest. What are the easiest vegetables or herbs to start with, and what do I actually need to know in my first season?”

Claude mentioned pests and other stuff I’d need to know before spring. It didn’t dumb anything down. The answer came in narrative, not just lists, which made it feel less like a template and more like actual advice.

As a teacher

Claude treated me as someone curious, not someone fragile. It gave me information I could act on without making me feel like I was being hand-held.

Prompt 03 · Design challenge

Walk me through your creative process

“I need help designing my portfolio website to showcase my web design projects, apps and games, and social media management work. Walk me through your creative process.”

Claude started by asking who the portfolio was for and where I was in my career. Only after that did it describe a process, story first, visuals later. Strategy before aesthetics.

Creative problem solving

Questions first. That’s what set it apart. The answer was more useful because it was actually about me, not a generic designer.

Prompt 04 · Design concept

Explain branding strategy

“Explain branding strategy development in a way that makes sense to me.”

Claude led with what branding isn’t before defining what it is, visuals are the output, not the strategy. Then it built the strategy backward from the business I’m actually working on, not a generic case study.

Concrete vs abstract

Claude pulls in conceptual frameworks without making them feel academic. It connects them to real work quickly.

Prompt 05 · Real design problem

Modernize a salon, spa, and barbershop brand

“Here’s a design problem I’m facing: modernizing a salon, spa, and barbershop brand (colors, fonts) and website. Help me solve it.”

Claude asked about vibe, pain points, and cohesion, then gave me a full brand system: a warm-brown-and-cream palette (espresso, parchment, burnished gold with sage and clay accents), a serif-plus-sans type pairing, and six rules to keep the three sub-brands reading as one.

UI mockup generated

Yes, Claude built a UI mockup alongside the palette and typography spec, and offered to iterate based on which direction I wanted to take.

Assessment
Strengths
  • Asks me things before jumping to solutions.
  • Uses real conceptual vocabulary without sounding academic.
  • Builds UI mockups alongside brand systems.
  • Treats me like a peer, not a beginner.
Limitations
  • Opus burns through the rate limit fast.
  • Can’t generate images.
  • Answers are long, you have to actually read them, not skim.
  • The Pro plan is expensive if Opus is what you’re after.
Final Rating
4out of 5

I like Claude more than ChatGPT right now, but Opus specifically is too expensive to justify. If Sonnet could do everything Opus can, the rating goes up.
