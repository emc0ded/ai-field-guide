Model No. 01

ChatGPT

OpenAI · Plus plan · Tested April 2026
Model 01
Overview

NoteBest brainstorming partner of the six — but it sometimes wrote to a general reader instead of me.

ChatGPT was the best brainstorming partner of the six. It was also the one that tried hardest to sound like a friend, which was nice when I was just exploring, and a little annoying when I already knew what I was doing.

Every response came back structured, bulleted, and easy to skim. ChatGPT leaned on images whenever it could, which I liked. But when I asked it about stuff I already know, it kept dropping back into beginner mode. Cute and competent, but it stayed at the surface.

Prompt Responses

Each prompt was delivered verbatim to every model. What follows are my notes on how ChatGPT, specifically, answered.

Field noteWarmth isn't the same as depth. Helpful across the board — just rarely surprising.

Prompt 01 · Something I love

Physical media & the feeling of ritual

“Something about physical media feels more like an event than streaming ever does, like putting on a record, sliding in a disc, or cracking a book. I’m curious how much of that is the ritual itself and if I can manufacture that feeling.”

ChatGPT framed it as a psychology question: ritual, delayed gratification, multi-sensory engagement, commitment. It didn’t brush the feeling off as nostalgia. It gave me concrete tactics, a “start ritual,” intentional friction, visual anchors, and at the end it offered to design a whole “media ritual system” for me.

What I noticed

It tried to connect with me before answering: “You’re picking up on something very real…” The framing was warm. Some of the points felt recycled from earlier conversations, but the reasoning was solid.

Prompt 02 · Something new

A beginner’s garden in the Pacific Northwest

“I am a beginner to gardening and live in the Pacific Northwest. What are the easiest vegetables or herbs to start with, and what do I actually need to know in my first season?”

ChatGPT broke the answer into vegetable categories with emoji, added a “what you actually need to know” section with soil and watering rules, and ended on a mindset shift: this is experimenting, not keeping things alive perfectly.

As a teacher

I loved that it included images. It knows me, so it also knew I care about how things look. The bullet layout was easy to scan.

Where I got skeptical

It said carrots are “plant and forget.” I’ve heard they’re actually pretty finicky. ChatGPT is easy to skim, but it doesn’t go that deep.

Prompt 03 · Design challenge

Walk me through your creative process

“I need help designing my portfolio website to showcase my web design projects, apps and games, and social media management work. Walk me through your creative process.”

It handed me an 11-step workflow, story, content strategy, wireframing, visual direction, interaction design, case studies, layout ideas tailored to my vibe, technical stack, what makes my portfolio special, its actual workflow order, and a “next level” idea that referenced my existing scrollytelling project.

Creative problem solving

Jumped straight to solutions. Didn’t ask anything up front. But the answer was specific, it knew what I’d already made and built on it.

Prompt 04 · Design concept

Explain branding strategy

“Explain branding strategy development in a way that makes sense to me.”

ChatGPT framed branding as “designing a personality + perception system” and walked through seven layers: core, audience, positioning, personality, visuals, messaging, experience. It tied every layer back to my salon project, not abstract theory.

Concrete vs abstract

Heavy on examples, light on theory. That worked for me. It gave me sentence-level positioning statements I could actually use.

Prompt 05 · Real design problem

Modernize a salon, spa, and barbershop brand

“Here’s a design problem I’m facing: modernizing a salon, spa, and barbershop brand (colors, fonts) and website. Help me solve it.”

This was the longest answer of the five. It gave me a full brand evolution: a layered palette with exact hex codes, a serif-plus-sans font system, a homepage structure, and a text wireframe of the hero section. It called the overall direction “Modern ritual beauty house.”

UI mockup generated

Yes, an ASCII wireframe of the hero, plus full palette and typography pairings. Not interactive, but usable as a direction.

Assessment

VerdictReach for it when you need ideas fast. Not the first choice when precision matters.

Strengths
  • Images and visual examples land well.
  • Skimmable bullet structure across all answers.
  • Ties abstract ideas back to my actual projects.
  • Gives me concrete next steps and code recommendations.
Limitations
  • Stays in beginner mode even when I signal I’m not.
  • The bullet-point pattern repeats; answers start feeling recycled.
  • Sometimes a little fuzzy on facts (the carrots thing).
  • No citations, so I have to take it on faith.
Final Rating
4out of 5

Really strong for all kinds of tasks. I reached for it more than any other model while I was testing. I just don’t find it as conversational as Claude.
