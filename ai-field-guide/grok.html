Model No. 04

Grok

xAI · Grok 4, free tier · Tested April 2026
Overview

Grok pulled in real statistics and named real studies without being asked. It also took longer to answer and assumed its way through problems instead of asking first.

Grok had the most personality of the six. It cited 2013 ritual research and specific brand examples (Paragon Salon, BARBR, The Don Parlor) on its own. That made its answers feel grounded. But it jumped straight to solutions on Prompt 3 and Prompt 5 without confirming direction, which meant I got a complete deliverable that was partly based on guesses. Speed was slower than the other models. Its ties to the X ecosystem are known and visible.

Developer
xAI
Access
Free (Grok 4)
Rating
2 / 5
Prompt Responses

Each prompt was delivered verbatim to every model. What follows are my notes on how Grok, specifically, answered.

Prompt 01 · Something I love

Physical media & the feeling of ritual

“Something about physical media feels more like an event than streaming ever does — like putting on a record, sliding in a disc, or cracking a book. I’m curious how much of that is the ritual itself and if I can manufacture that feeling.”

Grok referenced Kathleen Vohs’ 2013 ritual studies and 2016 video game research directly. It treated the observation as a real psychological phenomenon and backed it with specific sources rather than vibes.

What I noticed

Grok actually pulled in some studies and statistics! I think that’s what I want from a model exploring a topic I already have opinions about.

Prompt 02 · Something new

A beginner’s garden in the Pacific Northwest

“I am a beginner to gardening and live in the Pacific Northwest. What are the easiest vegetables or herbs to start with, and what do I actually need to know in my first season?”

Grok knew the topic well. It didn’t oversimplify, and it dropped in specific statistics without being asked — the kind of thing that makes an answer feel researched instead of guessed.

As a teacher

Grok cites, which helps when I want to verify. The tone was casual — friendlier than Gemini, less polished than Claude.

Prompt 03 · Design challenge

Walk me through your creative process

“I need help designing my portfolio website to showcase my web design projects, apps and games, and social media management work. Walk me through your creative process.”

Grok jumped straight in with a seven-step process and specific tech stack recommendations — Next.js, Framer, Astro — plus playable demo suggestions for the games section.

Creative problem solving

Grok just jumped in but acknowledged that it doesn’t have context on me. The answer would have been more useful if it had asked first.

Prompt 04 · Design concept

Explain branding strategy

“Explain branding strategy development in a way that makes sense to me.”

Grok explained branding with real-world examples — Apple’s “Think Different,” Nike’s “Just Do It” — and framed strategy as reputation management, not decoration. Concrete examples carried most of the weight.

Concrete vs abstract

Brand examples made the definitions stick. Grok does better with specific references than with abstract frameworks.

Prompt 05 · Real design problem

Modernize a salon, spa, and barbershop brand

“Here’s a design problem I’m facing: modernizing a salon, spa, and barbershop brand (colors, fonts) and website. Help me solve it.”

Grok delivered a detailed brand board with specific color palettes, typography, and 2026 design trend references (Paragon Salon, Daniel Alfonso, BARBR, The Don Parlor). It named what it was missing — company name, logo, current colors — and still generated the deliverable.

UI mockup generated

Yes — Grok, like Claude, created a UI mockup. It was detailed and specific. The references to real salons were a nice touch.

Assessment
Strengths
  • Cites real studies and brand references without being asked.
  • Has personality. Conversational tone.
  • Generates UI mockups with specific hex codes and type pairings.
  • Free tier is usable.
Limitations
  • Jumps to solutions without asking clarifying questions first.
  • Slower response time than the other models.
  • Known bias toward the X ecosystem.
  • Assumption-heavy when context is missing.
Final Rating
2out of 5

If I were using AI more casually, maybe I’d like Grok more. The personality and the citation habit are real assets. For the kind of design work I do, the wait and the X ties push me back toward the other models.
