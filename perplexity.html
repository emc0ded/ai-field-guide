Model No. 06

Perplexity

Perplexity AI · Free tier · Tested April 2026
Model 06
Overview

NoteShows its sources readily. Doesn't always know which ones matter — citation isn't the same as credibility.

Perplexity cited everything. The citations felt kind of arbitrary. Turns out showing sources isn’t the same as being trustworthy, and I’m not sure the sources were actually helping.

Perplexity is built for research. That’s where it should win, and in theory, source transparency is exactly what I want. In practice, the citations often pointed at generic web pages that didn’t really back up the specific claim being made. The creative work suffered for the same reason: structure without conviction. It wasn’t wrong. It wasn’t memorable either.

Prompt Responses

Each prompt was delivered verbatim to every model. What follows are my notes on how Perplexity, specifically, answered.

Field noteCitation-forward in every response. Useful as a starting point — less useful as a thinking partner.

Prompt 01 · Something I love

Physical media & the feeling of ritual

“Something about physical media feels more like an event than streaming ever does, like putting on a record, sliding in a disc, or cracking a book. I’m curious how much of that is the ritual itself and if I can manufacture that feeling.”

Perplexity acknowledged the role of ritual, cited some psychology, and offered practical setups for recreating it. Clean output. Not really original.

What I noticed

The answer read like a summary of other answers. Fine. Forgettable.

Prompt 02 · Something new

A beginner’s garden in the Pacific Northwest

“I am a beginner to gardening and live in the Pacific Northwest. What are the easiest vegetables or herbs to start with, and what do I actually need to know in my first season?”

Perplexity did cite some stuff, but it looked like it was pulled from random pages, not anything I’d actually trust. The answer was basic. Less helpful than ChatGPT, less grounded than Grok.

As a teacher

Citations were there, but they were weak. If I’m going to verify what a model says, I want the source to actually be worth checking.

Prompt 03 · Design challenge

Walk me through your creative process

“I need help designing my portfolio website to showcase my web design projects, apps and games, and social media management work. Walk me through your creative process.”

Perplexity gave me a skeleton: identity, structure, style. It was a fine outline. There wasn’t much creative voice under it, which mattered on a prompt that was specifically about creative process.

Creative problem solving

No questions. The process was generic and could’ve applied to anyone. That’s not what this prompt was asking for.

Prompt 04 · Design concept

Explain branding strategy

“Explain branding strategy development in a way that makes sense to me.”

Perplexity defined branding through a standard framework: foundation, research, positioning, personality, visuals, messaging, guidelines, testing. Thorough. Also generic.

Concrete vs abstract

The framework was right. The examples weren’t tied to my work, so the answer stayed at the definition level.

Prompt 05 · Real design problem

Modernize a salon, spa, and barbershop brand

“Here’s a design problem I’m facing: modernizing a salon, spa, and barbershop brand (colors, fonts) and website. Help me solve it.”

Perplexity gave me a color and typography system across three directions, plus a practical website checklist, booking-first, mobile responsive, social proof. At the end it asked which direction felt closest. No mockup.

UI mockup generated

No mockup. It offered to make one if I gave it more details, but it didn’t lead with a visual.

Assessment

VerdictUse it to fact-check a single claim. For anything more complex, look elsewhere.

Strengths
  • Source transparency, in theory.
  • Clean, organized output.
  • Practical checklists for design-adjacent work.
  • Free tier is usable for quick research lookups.
Limitations
  • Citations sometimes point at sources that don’t really back the claim.
  • Weaker on creative work than ChatGPT, Claude, or Llama.
  • No mockups.
  • No questions until the end.
Final Rating
2out of 5

I wasn’t really impressed. If I wanted a fact-check on a single claim, Perplexity might still be my first stop. For anything else, I reach for Claude or ChatGPT.
