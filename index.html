The AI Field Guide

Six large language models, five prompts, one design student with opinions. I ran every test myself and wrote down what I actually noticed, not what the models say about themselves.

On this guide

This isn’t a product comparison. It’s a field study. The six models behaved differently when I asked them the same thing, and the differences felt worth writing down.

I gave ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Grok, Llama, and Perplexity the same five prompts, pulled from my actual life as a design student. A reflection on physical media, a beginner question about gardening, a walkthrough of my creative process, a branding strategy explainer, and a real rebrand I was working on. Same prompt, same day, fresh chat for each one. Then I jotted down what I noticed about tone, depth, assumptions, and whether the answer was actually useful.

What came back was way more uneven than the marketing makes it sound. Some models asked me a question before answering. Some jumped straight in. Some cited sources that felt made up. Some were warm, some were robotic, one loved emojis a little too much. This guide is just me writing down what I saw.

The Method

Five prompts. One researcher. Notes taken as I went.

Each model answered the same five questions. I picked the prompts to test different kinds of thinking, personal reflection, beginner guidance, creative process, abstract concepts, and a live design problem I was actually stuck on.

I was watching for three things in every response: how it handled ambiguity, whether it asked me anything before answering, and whether what came back was something I could actually use.
