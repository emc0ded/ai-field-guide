The AI Field Guide

Six large language models, five prompts, one design student with opinions. I ran every test myself and wrote down what I actually noticed — not what the models claim about themselves.

On this guide

This is not a product comparison. It is a field study. The models behaved differently when asked the same thing, and the differences were worth documenting.

I tested ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Grok, Llama, and Perplexity against five prompts drawn from my actual life as a design student — a reflection on physical media, a beginner question about gardening, a creative process walkthrough, a branding strategy explainer, and a real rebrand problem I was solving. Each model got the exact same prompt. I logged the output, then wrote down what I observed about tone, depth, assumptions, and usefulness.

What came back was uneven in ways the marketing never admits. Some models asked clarifying questions before answering. Some jumped to solutions. Some cited sources that felt made up. Some were warm; some were robotic; one loved emojis. This guide documents those differences.

The Method

Five prompts. One researcher. Notes taken in real time.

Each model answered the same five questions. The prompts were chosen to test different kinds of thinking — personal reflection, beginner guidance, creative process, abstract concepts, and a live design problem.

I was looking for three things in every response: how it handled ambiguity, whether it asked clarifying questions, and whether it offered something I could actually use.
