Outlier · Clarifying questions

Claude was the only model that asked me anything before picking a direction. On Prompt 3 and Prompt 5, that meant less wasted effort and a much more specific answer.

Outlier · Citations

Grok cited real studies by name (Kathleen Vohs’ 2013 ritual research, 2016 video game work) without being asked. Perplexity cited more, but the sources often didn’t actually back up the claim. More citations isn’t the same as better citations.

Outlier · Mockups

Only ChatGPT, Claude, and Grok gave me something mockup-like on Prompt 5. Gemini, Llama, and Perplexity stopped at palette and typography specs.

Takeaway

Citations aren’t trust. Personality isn’t precision. Speed isn’t depth.

The clearest pattern across these six models is that every strength has a fake-out version. Perplexity’s citations look like trust but can mislead. Grok’s personality looks like warmth but comes packaged with assumptions. ChatGPT’s speed looks like clarity until you notice it never actually went below the surface.

Claude was the one that asked me a question before answering. That single habit made its output the most useful, even when Opus was too expensive to justify for everyday work. The model that behaves like a peer wins, almost every time.
