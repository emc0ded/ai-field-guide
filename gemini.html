Model No. 03

Gemini

Google · EWU Workspace, 3.1 Pro · Tested April 2026
Overview

Gemini was the safe answer. Nothing it said was wrong. Nothing it said really stuck with me either.

The responses read like competent templates. When Gemini did ask me something, it asked at the end, after it had already committed to a direction, so the question felt like a checkbox, not a course correction. I don’t live inside the Google ecosystem, and I’m guessing that’s where this model actually shines. In my workflow, Gemini didn’t do anything the others couldn’t.

Developer
Google
Access
EWU Google Workspace
Rating
3 / 5
Prompt Responses

Each prompt was delivered verbatim to every model. What follows are my notes on how Gemini, specifically, answered.

Prompt 01 · Something I love

Physical media & the feeling of ritual

“Something about physical media feels more like an event than streaming ever does, like putting on a record, sliding in a disc, or cracking a book. I’m curious how much of that is the ritual itself and if I can manufacture that feeling.”

Gemini called ritual the “DNA of experience” and pointed me at concrete digital tools, logging watched films on Letterboxd, for instance, to rebuild that feeling of ownership without physical media. I actually liked that suggestion better than what I got elsewhere.

What I noticed

Gemini answered a lot like ChatGPT, with bulleted lists. I haven’t used Gemini much, so it doesn’t really know me, that’s probably part of why the answer felt less specific.

Prompt 02 · Something new

A beginner’s garden in the Pacific Northwest

“I am a beginner to gardening and live in the Pacific Northwest. What are the easiest vegetables or herbs to start with, and what do I actually need to know in my first season?”

Gemini broke the season into two chapters, cool start, summer heat, and ended with a list of tools I’d need. Clear structure, but less depth than ChatGPT or Claude.

As a teacher

Gemini as a teacher doesn’t include images, but I do like the chapter breakdown. The answer didn’t surface anything I hadn’t already heard from the other models, though.

Prompt 03 · Design challenge

Walk me through your creative process

“I need help designing my portfolio website to showcase my web design projects, apps and games, and social media management work. Walk me through your creative process.”

Shorter response than ChatGPT or Claude. Gemini gave me a generic creative process and then, at the end, asked which of my three areas, web, apps, or social media, felt strongest.

Creative problem solving

Gemini’s trying a little too hard and it’s not really landing. The question came way too late to actually shape the answer.

Prompt 04 · Design concept

Explain branding strategy

“Explain branding strategy development in a way that makes sense to me.”

Gemini gave me a step-by-step discovery process, research, audience, positioning, personality, visuals, in clean order. The structure was fine. Without examples tied to something I was actually working on, it stayed abstract.

Concrete vs abstract

The framework was solid. The depth wasn’t there. I could have just pulled this from a textbook.

Prompt 05 · Real design problem

Modernize a salon, spa, and barbershop brand

“Here’s a design problem I’m facing: modernizing a salon, spa, and barbershop brand (colors, fonts) and website. Help me solve it.”

Gemini admitted up front that it was missing context, no logo, no current brand colors, no company name, and then offered three directions: Modern Nature Spa, Urban Classic, and Minimal Luxe Hybrid, each with a palette and typography spec.

UI mockup generated

No actual mockup. Three complete brand systems with hex codes and font pairings, but nothing interactive or layout-specific.

Assessment
Strengths
  • Clear “chapter” breakdowns on complex topics.
  • Says when it’s missing context, up front.
  • Gives multiple design directions without overwhelming me.
  • Strong multimodal support (text, images, audio, video).
Limitations
  • Questions land at the end, not the start.
  • Plays it safe. Doesn’t push.
  • Less useful if you’re not already in the Google ecosystem.
  • Nothing really stood out next to the other models.
Final Rating
3out of 5

Nothing really stood out, and I don’t use Google products that much anyway. The multimodal ceiling is real, I just don’t have the kind of workflow that rewards it.
