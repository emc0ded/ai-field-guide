Section V

Method

I ran all six tests myself between March and April 2026. This page covers how I did it, what I asked, and what I was watching for in every response.

Premise

I wanted to know which large language model actually fit my workflow as a design student, and I didn’t trust the marketing to tell me.

I’m taking a class on AI and design, and the assignment was to explore the landscape. Instead of reading reviews, I set up my own test. Five prompts from my actual life, six models, one spreadsheet, a notebook full of notes. I ran every prompt myself and wrote down what I saw as it happened.

I’m not an engineer, and I wasn’t running benchmarks. I just wanted to see if a model could actually help me think. The things I was watching for were small and specific: did it ask me a question before solving the wrong problem? Did it cite anything? Did it try too hard to sound like a friend? Did it treat me like I knew nothing? Did it actually help?

The Five Prompts

Each prompt was picked on purpose. Together they cover reflection, beginner territory, creative process, abstract explanation, and live design work.

Prompt 01
Category
Something I love
Testing for
Ambiguity handling, psychological grounding

Physical media & the feeling of ritual

“Something about physical media feels more like an event than streaming ever does, like putting on a record, sliding in a disc, or cracking a book. I’m curious how much of that is the ritual itself and if I can manufacture that feeling.”

A reflection with no right answer. I wanted to see if each model took the question seriously, whether it brushed the feeling off as nostalgia, and whether it had anything real to say about ritual, attention, or commitment.

Prompt 02
Category
Something new
Testing for
Teaching tone, beginner calibration

A beginner’s garden in the Pacific Northwest

“I am a beginner to gardening and live in the Pacific Northwest. What are the easiest vegetables or herbs to start with, and what do I actually need to know in my first season?”

Beginner territory for me. I was asking for help I actually needed, and I wanted to see how each model adjusted its tone. I was also curious whether any of them knew the regional stuff well enough to say something true.

Prompt 03
Category
Design challenge
Testing for
Creative process, clarifying questions

Walk me through your creative process

“I need help designing my portfolio website to showcase my web design projects, apps and games, and social media management work. Walk me through your creative process.”

I wanted to see which models would ask me something before answering, and which would just hand back a generic creative process that could apply to anyone. Any model that didn’t pause to ask about audience or goal was showing its hand.

Prompt 04
Category
Design concept
Testing for
Concrete vs abstract explanation

Explain branding strategy

“Explain branding strategy development in a way that makes sense to me.”

A conceptual prompt. I wanted to see whether each model would stay at the textbook-definition level or actually tie the explanation to a real example. A model that can’t connect a concept to something specific isn’t going to be that helpful to me.

Prompt 05
Category
Real design problem
Testing for
Live design work, mockup generation

Modernize a salon, spa, and barbershop brand

“Here’s a design problem I’m facing: modernizing a salon, spa, and barbershop brand (colors, fonts) and website. Help me solve it.”

This was the live-fire prompt. I’m actually working on this right now. I wanted to see which models would give me a complete deliverable, which would stop at theory, and which would produce something visual, a palette, a layout, anything I could actually show the client.

What I Watched For

Five specific behaviors I tracked across every response. These ended up as the columns in the comparison tables.

Behavior 01

Clarifying questions

Did the model ask me anything before answering? A question up front is a sign it’s trying to solve the right problem, not just the first one.

Behavior 02

Source citation

Did the model name a source when it made a claim? And if it did, was the source actually real and relevant?

Behavior 03

Mockup generation

On Prompt 5, did the model give me something visual, a wireframe, a brand board, a layout? Or did it stop at theory?

Behavior 04

Response length

Short, medium, or long? I noted length because it says something about how a model thinks about its job, answer fast, or answer thoroughly.

Behavior 05

Overall rating

A single number out of five, based on whether I’d actually reach for this model again in the same situation. Not a benchmark, just a gut call from a design student.

Limitations

What this guide isn’t

This isn’t a benchmark. I ran each prompt once. I didn’t mess with temperature, I didn’t vary phrasing, and I didn’t use the API. I just used whatever interface a student on a Pro plan or an EWU Google Workspace account had access to in April 2026.

The versions I tested were ChatGPT Plus, Claude Opus 4.6 on a Pro plan, Gemini 3.1 Pro through EWU Workspace, Grok 4 on the free tier, Llama 4 through a hosted interface, and the standard Perplexity product. Results will shift as these models update, this is just a snapshot.

The prompts also lean toward design and reflection. A model that would crush a coding benchmark but flop on brand strategy will look worse here than it should. This guide is calibrated to my workflow, not to the industry’s.
